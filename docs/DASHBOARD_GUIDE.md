# Fantasy Football Dashboard Guide

> Living guide to the dashboard generated by `expected_wins.py` and the live matchup snapshots written by `live_logger.py`. I'll update the relevant section alongside feature changes before they're pushed. This is plain Markdown, so you can edit it directly at any time.

`expected_wins.py` is the dashboard source and generates `index.html`. `live_logger.py` writes the `live_week_*.json` history used by Matchup stats. Do not edit generated `index.html` as the durable source.

## Shared controls and navigation

The team acronym buttons beside the league title select that team across all team-selectable charts, including the current matchup containing that team, the power-rank history, the multiverse views, and the Trade Calculator’s own-team roster. This changes selections rather than removing other teams' data. Individual chart buttons can still be changed independently.

The URL's versioned `view` query parameter stores the main tab, each subtab, global and local team choices, ranking week, matchup/week, table sorts, pinned chart teams, hidden legend series/slices, opened model details, a clicked roster distribution, the Trade Calculator’s teams, selected players, and week range, and Suggested Trades team/position filters and sort. Suggested Trades opens its alternate presentation on a single click/tap; a double click/tap selects genuine suggestions. Its mode is not stored in the URL. Reloading or sharing the URL restores those choices; native Back/Forward gestures undo and redo intentional selections. Hover and automatic snapshot refreshes do not add history entries. `dashboard_state.js` is embedded into the generated page and is the durable source of this behavior.

All tables size columns to their content rather than fixed widths or stretched equal columns. Long headings wrap, numeric values remain visible, and PPG is shown beneath totals. On phones, standings use team acronyms with accessible full names, wide tables scroll internally with a scroll hint and sticky team/player identity column, and roster photos/names use a compact layout.

The restyled dashboard preserves the dark palette, team/series colors, and system font. Primary navigation stays visible while scrolling; rounded subtabs separate related views. At widths up to 700px, navigation and team selectors scroll horizontally with 44px touch targets. Every bar chart is horizontal at every screen width, including weekly position/source stacks, win/loss differentials, schedule luck, and multiverse distributions; chart height grows with the number of rows. The six-axis profiles lead Positional Breakdown; selected-team weekly acquisition breakdowns appear before league comparisons, and phone radar cards form a swipeable strip. Every chart legend uses line segments instead of boxes and omits zero-reference series. Mobile charts use wrapping HTML legend buttons that preserve URL-backed visibility and distinguish dashed lines. Tables remain content-sized inside scroll regions. Cards, trade layouts, and leaderboard badges adapt to the available width. Reduced-motion settings disable chart animation. `dashboard_mobile.css` and `dashboard_mobile.js` are embedded into the generated page. The [mobile readability plan and implementation status](MOBILE_READABILITY_PLAN.md) records remaining refinements and verification limits.

The [pre-redesign backup](DASHBOARD_BACKUP.md) preserves the previous source, generated page, and guide on a separate Git branch, with a local ZIP as well.

## Dashboard tabs

### League Stats

#### Standings

Shows each team's completed regular-season record, playoff position, points for/against, and point differential. The sortable Last 3 column, immediately after PCT, shows wins–losses–ties over up to three most recent completed games; it sorts by that recent win percentage. ESPN's weekly standings order supplies its tiebreak ordering. When divisions exist, the playoff preview promotes division winners before placing the remaining teams by that standings order.

For a team with wins $W$, losses $L$, ties $T$, and $G=W+L+T$ completed games:

$$\mathrm{PCT}=\frac{W+0.5T}{G},\qquad \mathrm{DIFF}=\mathrm{PF}-\mathrm{PA}$$

Games back is shown as:

$$\mathrm{GB}=\frac{(W_{leader}-W)+(L-L_{leader})}{2}$$

The playoff position is the projected seed, separate from ESPN rank. The divider appears only when sorted by position and marks the playoff cutoff. Clinching markers are appended to team names: `x` clinched a playoff berth, `y` clinched a division, `z` clinched a first-round bye, and `e` eliminated. The rules account for divisions and the number of playoff spots.

#### Power Rankings

Ranks teams after each completed week and for the current season. The score is a 0–100 weighted sum:

$$\mathrm{Power}=100\left(0.30R+0.30R_3+0.10P+0.10P_3+0.20Q\right)$$

Here $R$ is overall win percentage, $R_3$ is win percentage over the last three completed games, $P$ is min-max normalized season PPG, $P_3$ is min-max normalized PPG over those recent games, and $Q$ is min-max normalized quality rating. Ties count as half a win. The custom quality rating credits wins over above-average scoring opponents, penalizes losses to below-average scoring opponents, and weights each result by the opponent's record entering that week. It is an Elo-style quality measure, not a conventional Elo rating. The chart shows rank history; the table shows the selected week's rank and score, with a colored dot beside the team acronym instead of a separate color column.

#### Playoff Bracket

Projects playoff seeds from the current standings and uses in-progress regular-season scores when available. ESPN settings supply the playoff team count, divisions, seeding rule, matchup periods, round dates, and tier type. The display draws the winner bracket, including byes, and a consolation bracket or ladder where ESPN's format indicates one. This supports common variations such as different playoff sizes and divisional seeding; an unusual custom tier format may require a format-specific adjustment.

### Matchup stats

#### Upcoming Matchup

Week buttons run from earliest to latest, with the current week selected initially. Matchup buttons precede weeks. Starter tables show both ESPN projections and actual points, mirrored so opposing actual scores meet at the center; slot labels sit below names. Names shorten on phones and expand on desktop, where tables use the available width. Historical lineup data is rebuilt from ESPN box scores; absent snapshots remain explicitly unavailable.

Completed weeks hide the projected-team-total card. For the current week, `live_logger.py` records current starter actuals and projections alongside the win-probability history. The open page fetches that file every minute. The finish distribution sums points already scored and simulated remaining individual scores, using positive lognormal outcomes and empirical nonpositive history. Finished players have no remaining variance. Its displayed range spans each team's central 95% of draws. The team sum is not assumed exactly normal. Live win probability uses the same family of individual distributions; starter independence remains an approximation. Collection targets ten-minute win-probability observations during game windows; GitHub schedules can be delayed, so polling cannot guarantee fresh data every minute.

### Team Stats

#### Positional Breakdown

Aggregates starter fantasy points by player position (QB, RB, WR, TE, K, D/ST). A FLEX or other multi-position slot is attributed to the player's position. It starts with a six-axis radar view, followed by totals across teams, a selected team's weekly stack, and a sortable totals table. The weekly numeric axis starts at zero unless that selected team has negative positional scoring; only then does it extend below zero. Weekly position sums are:

$$P_{t,w,k}=\sum_{i\in\text{team }t,\,\text{week }w,\,\text{position }k}\mathrm{points}_{i,w}$$

#### Acquisitional Breakdown

Groups starter points by how the player joined that fantasy roster: drafted, traded, or waivers/free agency. It displays league totals, weekly selected-team totals, and a sortable table. Both acquisition charts have a numeric axis minimum of zero; negative contributions remain in the underlying table/data but are clipped by the chart range. Weekly source points are the sum of the starter points assigned to that acquisition source. ESPN draft and transaction history are used where available; unclassified acquisitions use a waiver/free-agent fallback.

#### Starters vs. Bench

Compares actual lineup points with bench points and the best lineup that could have been set from the players available that week. The optimal-lineup calculation assigns each non-IR player to at most one ESPN starter slot, respects slot eligibility, and maximizes total points while retaining the actual starter-slot mix.

$$\mathrm{LeftOnBench}_w=\max(0,\mathrm{Optimal}_w-\mathrm{Actual}_w)$$

The summary table reports season totals, per-game averages, and realization of the optimal lineup. The charts compare actual versus optimal lineup points and starter versus bench totals across teams. The candle/distribution chart has been removed; points left on the bench still inform the optimal-lineup calculations and leaderboard.

#### Win/Loss Differential

Shows weekly points scored minus points allowed, centered at zero and colored by matchup result. The summary chart reports the average absolute differential in wins to the right of zero and the negative average absolute differential in losses to the left of zero.

$$d_w=\mathrm{PF}_w-\mathrm{PA}_w$$

#### Rosters

Shows each selected team's current roster, including players currently on that roster whose earlier scoring weeks were spent on other fantasy teams. Bye weeks are excluded. Season PPG is the mean of a player's completed weekly scores; $\overline{\Delta}$ vs. Projections (PPG) is the mean of actual points minus the initial ESPN projection across weeks with a usable projection. `SD vs. Projections` is the modelled standard deviation of weekly actual-minus-ESPN-projection points, using every available completed week with both an actual and positive ESPN projection. A player-specific lognormal fit is used with at least two positive score/projection ratios; shorter histories use a pooled same-position prior, then a league-wide prior, when at least one usable player-week exists. The value remains blank when even those data are unavailable.

Hovering, focusing, or clicking an SD vs. Projections value opens the weekly scoring distribution. For each eligible week, the model forms the ratio $R_i=S_i/P_i$, where $S_i$ is actual fantasy score and $P_i$ is that week's ESPN projection. Positive ratios are fitted as $\log(R)\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$; observed zero and negative ratios are retained as an empirical component, because a lognormal curve cannot represent them. The mixture is $R\sim q\,\mathrm{Lognormal}(\mu,\sigma^2)+(1-q)F_{\leq0}$, where $q$ is the observed share of positive ratios and $F_{\leq0}$ is the empirical distribution of nonpositive ratios. The hover graph scales the ratio model by the selected ESPN projection, shows the positive lognormal density, plots all weekly actual scores as yellow dots, and marks the model mean and ESPN projection with dashed lines. Only weekly scores, ESPN projection, and model mean appear in the legend; the distribution is omitted. Bye weeks and zero-score weeks without recorded ESPN game-stat participation are excluded from the SD fit and score dots. Nonzero outcomes and recorded zero-point appearances remain, including documented early injury exits. A projection or fantasy starting slot alone does not establish NFL participation; an unrecorded zero-point appearance cannot be distinguished from an absence and is omitted. Current injury labels are not used to erase earlier games. Current injury status appears in smaller red text after the player name. The mean delta heading uses a CSS overbar over Δ. Its horizontal range is 0–50, expanding only to include observed negative or above-50 weekly scores. The displayed SD vs. Projections is calculated from this projection-scaled mixture across the player's historical ESPN projections, not from the raw sample SD of fantasy points. Specifically, for empirical projections $P$ and a ratio $R$ assumed independent of $P$, $\mathrm{SD}(S-P)=\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[P^2]\mathbb{E}[(R-1)^2]-\{\mathbb{E}[P]\mathbb{E}[R-1]\}^2}$. When individual history is short, the lognormal shape comes from a same-position pool, then a league-wide pool, while the player's own historical projections scale the error SD. D/ST players use NFL team logos when a team logo can be identified.

On phones, explicitly tapping an SD value opens a pinned bottom sheet with a visible Close button, safe-area padding, internal scrolling, a backdrop, keyboard focus containment, and Escape support. Closing returns focus to that player's SD control. Hover/focus previews remain nonmodal on desktop.

### Luck Stats

#### Luck

Expected wins compare a team's score with every other team's score in the same week, not only its scheduled opponent. For team score $s_{t,w}$ among $N$ league teams:

$$xW_{t,w}=\frac{\#(s_{j,w}<s_{t,w})+0.5\,\#(j\ne t,\;s_{j,w}=s_{t,w})}{N-1}$$

A scheduled win counts 1, a tie 0.5, and a loss 0. Expected wins sum weekly $xW$; schedule luck is:

$$\mathrm{Luck}_t=\mathrm{ActualWins}_t-\sum_w xW_{t,w}$$

The subtab includes cumulative actual and expected wins, a league luck comparison, and a heatmap of weekly expected win shares.

#### Win/Loss Quality

Measures how strong the opponent's score was relative to that week's league scoring distribution. For opponent score $o$, weekly league mean $\mu_w$, and standard deviation $\sigma_w$:

$$z_w=\frac{o-\mu_w}{\sigma_w}$$

An above-average opponent win earns $\max(z_w,0)q_w$; a loss to a below-average opponent contributes $-\max(-z_w,0)q_w$. Ties contribute zero. The weight is $q_w=0.75+0.5r_w$, where $r_w$ is the opponent's win percentage entering the matchup (0.5 when no prior record exists). The table reports average quality in wins, average quality in losses, and their net, plus opponent PPG in each outcome type.

#### Fantasy Multiverse

Keeps each team's actual weekly score fixed and samples 20,000 valid round-robin schedules. Each simulated schedule has every opponent once, then repeats the same early-round opponents to fill the regular season. Matchups are re-drawn for each simulation. It applies the league's division and configured tiebreak rules, then records wins, standings, and first-place outcomes; completed postseason scores can also be used to estimate playoff advancement and title frequencies.

The charts show a selected team's wins distribution, distributions for all teams, the actual schedule result and percentile, and each team's share of first-place finishes or simulated titles. The selected team's pie slice is offset with a brighter border while other slices are dimmed. These are schedule-variance simulations, not projections of future player scores.

### Trades

#### Trade History

Shows completed accepted trades by team. For each trade, player points are counted from the week after the trade through the last completed scoring week. The team's trade yield is:

$$\mathrm{NetPoints}=\mathrm{IncomingPoints}-\mathrm{OutgoingPoints},\qquad \mathrm{PointsPerWeek}=\frac{\mathrm{NetPoints}}{\mathrm{CompletedWeeksAfterTrade}}$$

The cumulative chart shows raw received-minus-sent points only, shaded green above zero and red below. Individual trade charts sum all received players into one blue line and all sent players into one orange line, shading the difference green/red. Start/bench markers are removed. Photos retain orange sent and blue received outlines. Team ratings use raw net points with the existing heuristic thresholds; historical trade data may be incomplete without ESPN authentication.

#### Trade Calculator

Select your team and a partner, then tap players in the two side-by-side rosters to construct a hypothetical trade. Selected outgoing players use orange accents; incoming players use blue. Rosters follow the matchup slot order, with bench and IR after starters. The roster columns remain side by side on phones, with compact player details and internal scrolling. Results use a mirrored analysis board matching the supplied layout: team summaries across the top, projected rosters on the left/right, and a selectable weekly balance table in the center. Clicking a week updates both rosters; a shared Before/After trade selector retains both comparisons. On phones the week table moves above the two compact roster columns. Results update immediately, and team/player/week selections are shareable through the URL and native Back/Forward.

The calculator uses remaining-week ESPN projections and the league's configured starter slots and player eligibility. Each week is optimized independently, before and after exchanging the selected players. Roster projected PPG is colored from zero (red) to the position’s highest current rostered season PPG (green), clamped at those endpoints. Smaller adjacent text shows the mean delta versus projections. A player may fill at most one eligible slot; repeated RB/WR slots, FLEX, and superflex compete for the same players. Byes and known current-week absences contribute zero. After optimizing roster players, vacant slots are filled by a separate optimal assignment of currently available free agents; each replacement can fill only one slot. Their weekly projections, eligibility, byes, and current absences are respected. Occupied roster slots remain fixed. Before and after use the same free-agent pool, independently per team/week. Replacement identities are hidden in the lineup under the label “Replacement,” with projected points; unavailable free-agent data is disclosed in the method notes. Bench depth adds projected lineup points only when it improves an actual starting slot in one of the evaluated weeks.

The assignment below describes roster optimization; its total is then augmented by the best eligible free-agent assignment to any vacant slots. For roster $R$, week $w$, starter slots $S$, and projection $p_{i,w}$, the optimizer solves a maximum-weight assignment:

$$V(R,w)=\max_x\sum_{i\in R}\sum_{s\in S}p_{i,w}x_{i,s},\quad\sum_s x_{i,s}\leq1,\quad\sum_i x_{i,s}\leq1,\quad x_{i,s}=0\text{ if player }i\text{ is ineligible for slot }s.$$

For sent players $A$, received players $B$, and selected remaining weeks $W$, your gain is:

$$\Delta_w=V((R\setminus A)\cup B,w)-V(R,w),\qquad\Delta_{\mathrm{total}}=\sum_{w\in W}\Delta_w,\qquad\Delta_{\mathrm{per\ week}}=\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{total}}}{|W|}.$$

Each received player’s starting count is shown over the remaining evaluated weeks, including bye weeks in the denominator. The partner is evaluated with the inverse exchange. On a scheduled regular-season head-to-head week, each team’s balance subtracts the partner’s lineup gain: $B_{A,w}=\Delta_{A,w}-I_{A\text{ plays }B,w}\Delta_{B,w}$. Other weeks retain the own-lineup gain. Totals, trade ratings, and suggestions use this balance; before/after lineups still show raw projected points. Unknown playoff opponents are not assumed. Own-lineup and opponent-effect totals are displayed separately. Ratings use each team’s average weekly gain: within ±0.5 points/week is even; two positive changes are mutually beneficial, two negative changes mutually detrimental, two even changes even, and all other combinations one-sided. Directional arrows show both teams’ changes. Trading a 15-point starting RB for a 20-point WR who cannot improve any WR/FLEX slot can therefore reduce your projected lineup total. Per-week results and before/after lineups expose the replacement player and bye coverage rather than presenting the sum of traded-player PPG as value.

Projection source and availability information accompanies the forecast. Future weeks use ESPN's week-specific projections when present; any fallback baseline is labeled, and missing projections are not treated as a confident zero-point forecast. Current injury designations are not assumed permanent: later weeks are conditional on availability, as described in the calculator. This is a projected optimal-lineup comparison, not a trade acceptance model or a forecast of actual lineup decisions. Replacement candidates include available free agents and waiver-wire players and assume current availability persists; waiver priority and claim outcomes are not predicted; they are hypothetical, not actual acquisitions. It does not enforce transaction/roster limits, predict future injuries, or submit trades to ESPN. Its inputs refresh when the dashboard is rebuilt.

#### Suggested Trades

This subtab searches candidate packages of one to four players per side for the selected team, using the same weekly lineup, replacement, bye, and scheduled-opponent model. It evaluates every 1-for-1 exchange, then expands a small diverse set of promising candidates for each package size. Projected package totals prioritize expansions, but the full weekly lineup model evaluates every displayed result. This bounded search supports unequal packages and 4-for-4 trades; it does not exhaustively enumerate all combinations or guarantee a globally optimal trade. Computation runs in batches and results are cached per team.

Partner-team and player-position buttons allow multiple selections simultaneously. Selected partners are combined with OR. Position filters are exclusionary: every player on both sides of a trade must belong to a selected position. For example, selecting RB and WR excludes any package containing a QB, TE, K, or D/ST. The team and position filters are combined with AND. All clears that filter group and allows every team or position. Column buttons sort results, defaulting to highest own balance gain; at most 150 results are shown after filtering and sorting. Every player in a package is listed individually with their position.

There is no code prompt. Single clicking/tapping Suggested Trades opens the deliberately misleading alternate presentation by default. Double clicking or tapping twice within 500 ms selects genuine mutually beneficial suggestions, requiring both teams to gain at least 0.5 balance points per remaining week. The alternate presentation retains only exchanges where each team loses 0.1–3 points/week and displays values as $1.12|B|+0.1$. This alternate presentation is not truthful trade advice. It never alters the calculator, underlying projections, or trades. Neither branch invents trades when none meet its criteria. Both presentations have identical layouts. The interaction is client-side presentation logic, not authentication, and reload/history entry defaults to the alternate presentation.

### Leaderboard

Shows the top and bottom team for four current-season categories: highest total points scored, schedule luck, points left on the bench per game, and summed trade points per week. Higher is better for scoring, luck, and trade value; lower is better for points left on the bench. These are descriptive season awards, not predictive ratings.

## Research

### Market-informed player projection research plan

The goal is to test whether pregame NFL market information improves fantasy-point projections under this league's ESPN scoring rules. It is a model-quality study, not a betting tool. ESPN's data interface and community wrappers expose week-specific scoring periods and weekly projected/actual player scores, so historical season/week values are a promising starting point. Explicit season and scoring-period queries can access prior weeks, but ESPN's historical-league endpoint now requires an `espn_s2` cookie, and private-league access may require the existing ESPN credentials. Retrieved projections do not guarantee the exact value published before kickoff: ESPN projections can be revised, historical endpoints can change, and the API does not provide a reliable point-in-time timestamp for each old value. The current lineup snapshots should therefore be archived with their capture timestamp and kickoff to create an auditable, true pregame projection history. See the [ESPN player-field reference](https://github.com/cwendt94/espn-api/wiki/Player-Class), [fflr weekly scoring-period documentation](https://k5cents.github.io/fflr/), and its [API access notes](https://k5cents.github.io/fflr/reference/ffl_api.html).

Historical spread and game-total data are available in nflverse's schedule/game data. Its documented `spread_line` is positive when the home team is favored, and `total_line` is the game total; nflfastR identifies these as closing lines sourced from Pro-Football-Reference. The documented Games data begins in 2006, which is enough for broad backtesting but is a single closing value, without a retained capture time or a line-movement history. It should be treated as a useful historical baseline, not a verified pre-kickoff snapshot at a chosen cutoff. See the [nflverse Games data definitions](https://github.com/nflverse/nfldata/blob/master/DATASETS.md?plain=1#L819-L881) and the [nflfastR release notes](https://github.com/nflverse/nflfastR/blob/master/NEWS.md).

1. **Audit the historical ESPN fields.** For several completed seasons, request one `seasonId` and `scoringPeriodId` at a time, then compare ESPN weekly projected and actual points against known completed weeks and this league's scoring settings. Record which player types, weeks, and past seasons return usable projected values. Label these as historical weekly projections unless their pre-kickoff timing can be independently established.
2. **Start an immutable projection archive.** Extend the existing scheduled workflow to save every player projection snapshot with season, week, ESPN player ID, NFL team, position, league scoring configuration, `captured_at`, and scheduled kickoff. Preserve every observation rather than overwriting it. For each model row, choose the latest capture strictly before that player's NFL kickoff, and exclude any post-kickoff value.
3. **Join market context.** Map player team and opponent to NFL game ID, season, and week. Use historical closing spread/total for initial broad analysis, clearly marked as closing-line features. For a strict as-of backtest, obtain and archive time-stamped line snapshots (including source/provider and capture time) so market and ESPN inputs use the same pregame cutoff. Convert game lines to implied team points with the nflverse sign convention: $\text{home implied points}=(\text{total}+\text{spread})/2$ and $\text{away implied points}=(\text{total}-\text{spread})/2$.
4. **Build and compare models.** Establish an ESPN-only baseline, then add implied team points and spread as features to a player-level projection model. Add position, player's recent role/opportunity, injury/availability, and scoring settings only when those fields were known at the same cutoff. Fit with season-forward training and temporal holdouts; tune by position and score component rather than randomly splitting player-weeks.
5. **Evaluate without leakage.** Reconstruct target fantasy points from NFL statistics and the exact ESPN league scoring rules. Compare MAE and RMSE against ESPN-only projections, and assess bias and interval calibration by position, projection range, and season. Verify all features were captured before kickoff and avoid postgame stats, closing lines mislabeled as early lines, or end-of-week ESPN revisions. Keep the simpler ESPN baseline if market features fail to improve held-out accuracy consistently.

The nflverse schedule is accessible and well suited to an initial exploratory analysis, but its closing spread/total alone cannot answer whether a line was available at a specific pregame moment. Capturing ESPN projection and market timestamps going forward is the key step for a trustworthy point-in-time training set.

### Existing weekly scoring study

This section summarizes the [full model study](../analysis/player_score_models/report.md). The reproducible scoring reconstruction uses public [nflverse player, team, snap-count, and play-by-play data](https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data) and this league's current [ESPN scoring settings](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360003914032-Scoring-Formats). It rescored 81,343 active regular-season player-games from 2015–2025; 2015–2023 trained the curves and 2024–2025 were held out. The 2026 league settings were applied retroactively.

The reconstruction matched 998 current ESPN rostered player-games; 98.7% were within 0.05 points, with 0.015 points mean absolute error. QB, RB, and TE matched exactly in the comparison. Remaining differences were concentrated in a small number of WR, kicker, and D/ST games.

### Why one normal curve is insufficient

Weekly scoring has a large exact-zero outcome for some positions, a separate negative tail from turnovers and defensive scoring, and a right-skewed positive outcome. On the 2024–2025 holdout set, normal fits to individual player-seasons substantially overestimated below-zero probabilities for RB, WR, and TE, while the observed exact-zero rates were high.

| Position | Exact zero | Negative | Under 1 point | Normal fit below 0 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| QB | 3.6% | 7.4% | 16.4% | 4.4% |
| RB | 14.8% | 1.4% | 24.4% | 11.4% |
| WR | 26.8% | 0.7% | 29.1% | 13.0% |
| TE | 40.3% | 0.2% | 41.1% | 15.7% |
| K | 2.3% | 0.9% | 3.4% | 4.0% |
| D/ST | 0.2% | 12.8% | 16.7% | 13.6% |

### Proposed player-level model

Use a three-part distribution with position- and player-informed negative scores, a point mass at zero, and a positive-score curve. Let $p_-$, $p_0$, and $p_+$ be the probabilities of negative, zero, and positive scores, with $p_-+p_0+p_+=1$. If $m_+$ is a player's typical score conditional on scoring positively and $Y$ is the normalized positive shape, then:

$$S\sim\begin{cases}\text{negative-score distribution},&p_-\\0,&p_0\\m_+Y,&p_+\end{cases}$$

The density transformation for positive scores is $f_S(s)=m_+^{-1}f_Y(s/m_+)$, for $s>0$. Gamma and lognormal-mixture curves are both positive-only and therefore cannot assign impossible negative values to the positive component. Estimate player-specific zero and negative rates from their own history and shrink them toward position baselines when the player sample is short.

### Held-out positive-score curve comparison

Scores above zero were divided by each player's positive-game mean before fitting. The table gives the candidate with the highest 2024–2025 held-out mean log score; differences are small for several positions, so the top model should be treated as a useful shape choice rather than a universal truth.

| Position | Best positive component | Mean log score | Observed / fitted share below 25% of positive mean |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| QB | Two-lognormal mixture | −0.741 | 7.7% / 6.9% |
| RB | Two-lognormal mixture; Gamma is essentially tied | −0.918 | 11.0% / 11.8% |
| WR | Gamma | −0.842 | 7.2% / 7.0% |
| TE | Two-lognormal mixture; Gamma is essentially tied | −0.815 | 4.5% / 5.8% |
| K | Two-lognormal mixture | −0.723 | 5.2% / 5.9% |
| D/ST | Two-lognormal mixture; Gamma is close | −0.890 | 8.9% / 10.3% |
